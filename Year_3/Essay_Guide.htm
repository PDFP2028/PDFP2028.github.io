Possible essay question #1:  Using philosophical language and categories, define and explain "eternal" as it applies to God. Be sure to include a discussion of His immateriality.  In other words, God as an infinite existence. Consider: (see Trinity Homework #1)

1.      God is eternal

a.      God is not “endless time” because He is outside of time.

b.      Eternity “all at once”

c.      Eternity is not time stretched endlessly backward and forward; it is the absence of time’s internal succession

d.      He possesses Himself wholly in one act of being; ALL AT ONCE (see Sheed page 28)

e.      God possesses the Divine Being without beginning and without end, and without succession in a constant, undivided now. (see Ott page 39)

f.        Above is not shabby at all.  If you want a more perfect score consider tying in His eternality with his immutability which you mention below.

g.       Also, extra brownie points if you mention a distinction in how we use the word eternal as in “he entered into eternal rest (died) on May 31 as applied to humans and how the same word is applied to God.  This would be top shelf work.

2.      God is immaterial

a.      God is not composed of physical matter and has no body, humans have bodies and parts

b.      He is incorporeal and spiritual

c.      Human knowledge is limited and cannot comprehend the fullness of God. This can be explained by what God is not: not a body, not changeable,

d.      Ok, so God does not have a body… does this fact play your points 1 and 3?  If so how?  

3.      God is an infinite existence

a.      Infinitely active yet utterly changeless, especially in His knowing and loving

b.      Without limits or boundaries – limitless – boundless

c.      The absence of all limitations, not only external but internal as well

d.      No external boundary, but also no internal limitations, no parts, and no divisions (see Sheed page 25-26)

e.      God is spirit, and God does not occupy space

f.        God is everywhere, not by spatial spread, but by real presence through the operation of His power – holding all things in existence

g.       Utterly immutable  (see Ott page 38)

h.      These are all good bullet points- how you flesh them out will make a difference (avoid saying the same thing in different ways- for instance is there a distinction between your point c and d?  If so what it is?)

 

 

 

 

Possible essay question #2: Using philosophical language and categories to define and explain the Persons of the Trinity as they relate to the operations of the intellect and will.  

The operations of the intellect (the power to think/reason) and will (the power to choose/love)

1.      Father

a.      God the Father, being a perfect thinker, has a perfect thought of Himself

b.      This thought is perfect, it possesses everything the Father has

c.      This is the Intellect of God

2.      Son

a.      This perfect expression of the divine intellect is the Second Person of the Trinity, Jesus Christ.

b.      This is the result of the Intellect of God (Generation)

3.      Holy Spirit

a.      The Will

b.      The Eternal Exchange: The Father looks at the Son and loves Him perfectly. The Son receives this love and gives it back.

c.      From the eternal exchange of love is so perfect that it “spirates” (breathes) the Third Person of the Trinity. (Spiration)

4.      Ok so what you have is good but to complete it, you would need to include a discussion on the distinction of persons.  How is it that the Son has everything the Father has except the Son is not the Father?  What items lend themselves to this understanding.

 

Depending on the time/ resources you have, you could pass both essays with what you have listed already (unless you botch the execution by making some grievous error or many small ones, etc) but to excel (and why not shoot for the stars) brush up on the suggested materials.  All the while keeping in mind, at the end of the day this essay is worth 20 points,  which is less than 1/6 of the total points available.
